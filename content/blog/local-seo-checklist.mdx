If someone searches "plumber Parramatta" or "family lawyer Geelong" right now, does your business show up in the map pack? If you're not certain, that's revenue walking straight to a competitor with a tidier Google Business Profile.

Local SEO is the one channel where ecommerce and service businesses genuinely need different plays. If you run a national online store, this isn't for you. But if you're a tradie, a clinic, a law firm, an accountant, a café, or any business that serves people in specific suburbs or cities, this checklist is the difference between being found and being invisible.

## Claim and complete your Google Business Profile properly

Most businesses claim their **Google Business Profile** (GBP) once and never touch it again. That's like printing a Yellow Pages ad in 2003 and assuming it still works.

Run through this:

- **Business name** matches your actual trading name exactly. No keyword stuffing ("Sydney's Best Plumber Pty Ltd") because Google penalises this and it looks desperate anyway.
- **Primary category** is the single most important field. A "Plumber" ranks for plumbing searches better than a profile wrongly set to "Contractor" with plumbing mentioned in the description.
- **Secondary categories** cover the specific services you actually offer, like "Hot water system supplier" or "Emergency plumber."
- **Service areas** list every suburb you genuinely cover, not just your postcode.
- **Hours** are accurate, including public holiday hours around Christmas and Easter, when "closed, unexpectedly" reviews do real damage.
- **Photos** get updated quarterly. Profiles with recent, real photos (not stock) get more engagement in Google's own reporting to business owners.
- **Products or services sections** list what you do with prices where possible. Service businesses skip this constantly and it's free real estate.

> **Benchmark:** Google's own guidance confirms that businesses with complete profiles are more likely to appear in local search and get more requests for directions and clicks to their website than incomplete ones.

## Build a review engine, not a review request

A profile with four reviews from 2021 tells searchers you might be closed. A profile with forty reviews, the most recent one from last week, tells searchers you're busy and trusted.

The mistake most businesses make is asking for reviews only when a job goes brilliantly. That gives you a thin, lumpy set of five-star reviews that looks staged. Instead, build the ask into your process:

1. **Trigger the request at the natural end point.** For a trade, that's job completion. For a service business, that's the final invoice or project wrap-up. For a clinic, it's the follow-up appointment.
2. **Make it a two-click process.** A direct link to your review form, sent by text or email, beats a vague "please review us" mention in person.
3. **Respond to every review, good and bad.** A thoughtful response to a three-star review often does more to build trust with prospective customers than another five-star one, because it shows how you handle problems.
4. **Never buy reviews or incentivise them with discounts.** Google can detect unnatural review patterns and it will suppress or remove them, which wastes the time you spent collecting them in the first place.

This is also where ecommerce and service businesses converge. A DTC brand with a physical showroom or studio still needs this exact system for that location.

## Build suburb-specific service pages, not suburb spam

This is where most local SEO advice online goes wrong. The old trick was to build a page for every suburb within 50km, swap the suburb name into an identical template, and wait for rankings. Google's algorithm updates over the past several years have specifically targeted this pattern, and thin, duplicated location pages now tend to get filtered rather than ranked.

The fix is to be genuinely useful at the suburb level:

- **Only build a page for suburbs you can say something real about.** If you've done 40 jobs in Blacktown, write about Blacktown. If you've done two, don't.
- **Include specific detail.** Local landmarks, strata requirements for apartment blocks in that area, typical turnaround times given local traffic or council rules. Generic copy is the tell that gives thin pages away.
- **Link service pages to your core offer pages**, not just to the homepage. A "Bathroom Renovations Blacktown" page should link to your main renovations service page, and vice versa.
- **Use real photos from real jobs in that suburb where you have them.** This does double duty as proof and as unique content Google can't find anywhere else.
- **Don't force a page for every postcode.** Ten strong, specific suburb pages outperform fifty thin ones every time, and they're far less work to maintain.

If you're deciding where to start, look at your last twelve months of completed jobs or clients by suburb and build pages for the top performers first. That's where you already have proof, photos and testimonials sitting in your files.

## Get your citations and directory listings consistent

**NAP consistency** (Name, Address, Phone) across directories like your website, GBP, industry directories and local business associations is a known trust signal. If your phone number on your website doesn't match the one on your Google profile, that inconsistency can quietly undermine confidence in the listing's accuracy.

Check your listing on at least the big generalist directories plus one or two industry-specific ones relevant to your field. Fix mismatches once a quarter. This is tedious, unglamorous work, and it's also one of the few local SEO tasks with a completely binary outcome: either it's consistent or it isn't.

## Track what actually matters

Vanity metrics like "profile views" feel good and tell you almost nothing. Track:

- **Calls and direction requests** from your Google Business Profile insights
- **Form submissions or booked calls** that can be attributed to organic search, not just paid traffic
- **Rank position** for your top five to ten suburb and service combinations, checked monthly rather than daily
