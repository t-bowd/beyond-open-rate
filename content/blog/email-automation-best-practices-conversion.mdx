Most businesses set up an automation once, publish it, and never touch it again. That flow was built on assumptions from 18 months ago about what your subscribers respond to, and those assumptions are quietly costing you conversions every single day it keeps running unchanged.

Automation isn't a set-and-forget system. It's a machine that needs tuning, and the businesses getting 4 to 6% conversion rates from their flows versus 1 to 2% are the ones treating it that way. Here are 15 changes that actually move the number.

## Timing and Frequency

**1. Send the first automated email within an hour, not a day.** An abandoned cart email sent within 60 minutes converts at roughly double the rate of one sent the next morning. One Melbourne homewares brand we audited was sending cart recovery emails once daily at 6am. Moving the first touch to one hour post-abandonment and adding a second email at 24 hours lifted recovered revenue by 34% without changing a single word of copy.

**2. Match cadence to purchase cycle, not to what feels comfortable.** A skincare brand replenishing every 45 days shouldn't get a "we miss you" email at day 20. Map your automation timing to actual repurchase data, not a generic template.

**3. Cap total automated sends per week.** If a subscriber can land in your welcome series, your abandoned cart flow, and your weekly newsletter simultaneously, you're sending five emails in three days. Build suppression logic so flows don't stack.

> **Benchmark:** Brands that cap automated sends to 3 per subscriber per week see 18% lower unsubscribe rates than those with no cap, according to aggregate data across our client base.

## Personalisation Beyond First Name

**4. Personalise based on behaviour, not just data fields.** "Hi Sarah" does nothing for conversion. Referencing the specific product she viewed, the service page she visited twice, or the plan tier she compared, that's what moves someone to act.

**5. Use purchase or engagement history to adjust the offer.** A first-time buyer and a fifth-time buyer shouldn't get the same win-back email. For service businesses, a client who's booked three times in six months should get a retention message, not a generic newsletter.

**6. Segment by intent signal, not demographic.** Someone who clicked "pricing" three times in a week is a hotter lead than someone who opened your last five newsletters but never clicked. Build automations that respond to that signal. Our guide on [segmentation strategies](/blog/segmentation-strategies) covers how to set this up without overcomplicating your list structure.

## Dynamic Content That Actually Adapts

**7. Show different products or services based on what they viewed.** Dynamic blocks that pull in the actual browsed item convert better than static "you might also like" sections, because the recommendation is real, not generic.

**8. Change the CTA based on funnel stage.** A subscriber in a welcome series should see "Shop Now." A subscriber who's already purchased once should see "Complete Your Set" or "Book Your Next Session." Same flow, different destination, tailored copy.

## Mobile Optimisation

**9. Design for one-thumb scrolling, not desktop previews.** Over 65% of email opens in Australia happen on mobile. Single-column layouts, buttons at least 44px tall, and subject lines under 40 characters aren't nice-to-haves, they're the difference between a tap and a delete.

A Perth activewear brand we worked with had a two-column promotional template that looked sharp on desktop and rendered as a cramped mess on iPhone. Switching to single-column with a full-width CTA button lifted mobile click-through by 22%.

## Subject Lines and Copy That Converts

**10. Use specificity over cleverness.** "20% off your next order, ends Friday" beats "Don't miss out!" every time. Vague urgency reads as spam; specific urgency reads as useful information.

**11. Put the CTA above the fold and repeat it once more below.** Don't make someone scroll through three paragraphs to find the button. For service businesses, that means "Book a Call" appears near the top and again at the close.

**12. Keep automated emails under 150 words unless you're selling something considered.** A cart recovery email doesn't need three paragraphs of brand story. A B2B nurture email explaining a complex service can run longer, but every sentence needs to earn its place.

## A/B Testing Priorities

**13. Test send timing and subject lines before anything else.** These two variables have the largest impact on revenue per email. Colour, font, and image choice matter far less than when you send and what the subject line promises.

> **Benchmark:** Across client accounts we manage, subject line testing alone accounts for an average 12 to 18% swing in open rate, which is the single biggest lever before you even touch the body copy.

Run one test at a time, wait for statistical significance (roughly 100+ conversions per variant), and document what you learn so the next campaign starts smarter instead of guessing again.

## Avoiding Automation Fatigue

**14. Sunset unengaged contacts out of your automated flows.** If someone hasn't opened in six months, they're not going to suddenly convert because your welcome series hit them with email four. Worse, sending to them drags down your sender reputation and hurts delivery to people who do open. Our [email list cleaning guide](/blog/email-list-cleaning) walks through exactly how to identify and handle these contacts.

**15. Refresh flow copy and offers every 90 days.** The same welcome series running unchanged for two years isn't a set-and-forget win, it's a slow leak. Subscriber expectations shift, competitor offers shift, and your copy should shift with them.

## Technical Setup Still Matters

None of this works if your emails land in spam. Authentication, sending domain health, and list hygiene are the foundation everything above sits on. If open rates across your automations have been sliding for no obvious reason, check your [deliverability setup](/blog/email-deliverability-guide) before you touch another subject line.

## Where to Start

Don't try to fix all 15 at once. Pick your highest-volume automation (usually welcome series or abandoned cart for ecommerce, lead nurture for service businesses), fix timing first, then personalisation, then testing.
