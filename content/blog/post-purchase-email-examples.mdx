Most stores send three emails after a purchase: confirmation, shipping notice, and maybe a review request if someone remembered to set it up. That's it. Then silence, until the next sale email blasts the whole list regardless of what anyone bought.

That's not a post-purchase sequence. That's the bare minimum required to not confuse the customer about where their parcel is.

> **Benchmark:** Brands running a full 8-to-10 email post-purchase sequence generate roughly 3x the revenue per customer over 90 days compared to brands sending only the transactional basics.

The 90 days after someone buys is the highest-intent window you'll ever have with that customer. They trust you enough to hand over their card details. Wasting that window on silence, or on a single "leave us a review!" email, is money you already earned and then declined to collect. We've covered the mechanics of building this out in the [post-purchase email flow guide](/blog/post-purchase-email-flow). This post is about what actually goes in each email.

## Email 1: Order Confirmation (Immediate)

This isn't marketing, it's a receipt. But it's also the highest open-rate email you'll ever send, often north of 60%, so don't waste it on "Thanks for your order!" and nothing else.

**Subject line:** "Order #4471 confirmed, here's what happens next"

**Copy angle:** Confirm the order, set delivery expectations, and add one soft nudge, a link to track the order or a note about your loyalty programme. Don't try to sell anything here. You'll wreck trust in the one email people actually read closely.

## Email 2: Shipping Notification (When It Ships)

Another high-open email. People want to know where their stuff is.

**Subject line:** "It's on the way: your order ships today"

**Copy angle:** Tracking link, expected delivery window, and one line of anticipation building, "here's what to do when it arrives" if there's an unboxing or setup step worth mentioning.

## Email 3: Delivery Check-In (1–2 Days After Delivery)

Most brands skip this one entirely. It's the difference between a transaction and a relationship.

**Subject line:** "How's the [product] treating you?"

**Copy angle:** No sales pitch. Ask if it arrived okay, link to a how-to or FAQ if the product has any setup involved, and give a reply-to address that actually gets checked. This email builds goodwill that pays off in the review and cross-sell emails that follow.

## Email 4: Review Request (3–5 Days After Delivery)

Time this to delivery date, not order date. Asking for a review the day the parcel ships is asking someone to review a box.

**Subject line:** "Got 30 seconds? We'd love your thoughts on [product]"

**Copy angle:** Make the ask specific and low-friction, one-click star rating before a written review. Offer a small incentive if your margins allow it, a discount code for the next order. Reviews you collect here become your ad creative and product page proof for the next twelve months.

## Email 5: Cross-Sell (Day 10–20)

Now you can sell, because you've earned some trust and the customer has had time to use the product.

**Subject line:** "Pairs perfectly with your [product]"

**Copy angle:** Recommend based on what they actually bought, not your bestsellers list. A customer who bought a coffee grinder wants filters and beans, not a blender. This is the email that turns a $60 order into a $150 customer.

## Email 6: Replenishment (Timed to Product Lifespan)

If you sell anything consumable, coffee, skincare, supplements, pet food, this email should be running on a timer matched to how long the product actually lasts.

**Subject line:** "Running low on [product]? Reorder in one click"

**Copy angle:** Reference the exact product and quantity they bought, make reordering a single click, and offer a subscribe-and-save option if you have one. This is one of the highest-converting emails in ecommerce because it targets people who already know they need the product.

## Email 7: Loyalty or Brand Story (Day 30–45)

This is where you build the relationship past the transaction. Not every email needs a discount attached.

**Subject line:** "The story behind [product] (and why we made it this way)"

**Copy angle:** Share founder story, sourcing details, sustainability angle, whatever makes your brand more than a logo on a box. Customers who feel connected to a brand have measurably higher repeat purchase rates than customers who just bought something cheap once.

## Email 8: Win-Back Trigger (Day 60–90, If No Repeat Order)

If the customer hasn't bought again by day 60 to 90, that's a signal, not a lost cause.

**Subject line:** "We miss you, here's 15% back"

**Copy angle:** Acknowledge it's been a while, offer a real incentive, and make it easy to reorder their previous item in one click. This is your last structured touch before they drop into general newsletter sends.

## The Service Business Equivalent

If you're running a service business rather than shipping boxes, the same logic applies to project completion instead of order delivery. A project wrap email replaces "delivery," a satisfaction check-in replaces the review request, and a "here's what else we can help with" email replaces the cross-sell.

The win-back trigger becomes a check-in at the point their retainer or contract would naturally renew. Agencies, consultants, and B2B SaaS companies that build this sequence turn one-off projects into repeat retainers, which is worth more than any single review.

None of this requires a developer. It requires someone deciding to write eight emails instead of three, and setting up the triggers correctly, delivery date for reviews, product-specific timing for replenishment, and 60 to 90 days of silence for win-back.
