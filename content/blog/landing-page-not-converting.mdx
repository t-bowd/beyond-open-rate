A landing page that isn't converting is rarely a mystery. It's usually one of nine specific, fixable problems, and most businesses never diagnose which one they've got. They just add more traffic and hope the maths works out.

It doesn't. A broken page converts the same percentage of 10,000 visitors as it does of 1,000. Before you spend another dollar on clicks, find out which of these nine is yours.

## Before the click: the page was dead on arrival

### 1. Message mismatch
The ad says "50% off summer dresses." The landing page headline says "Welcome to our store." That gap, even a small one, makes visitors feel like they clicked the wrong link and they bounce before reading anything else.

**Fix:** Match the headline on the page to the promise in the ad, almost word for word. If the ad mentions a discount, a product, or an offer, that exact thing should be the first thing the visitor sees.

### 2. Wrong traffic sent to the wrong page
Ecommerce brands often send every ad to the homepage. Service businesses send every enquiry to a generic "Contact Us" page. Neither explains anything specific, so the visitor has to do the work of figuring out if they're in the right place.

**Fix:** Build a dedicated page per offer or per service, not one page trying to serve everyone. A plumber running an emergency callout ad needs a page about emergency callouts, not the homepage with six services listed.

### 3. Load time
Visitors decide whether to stay before most pages have finished loading images.

> **Benchmark:** Google's own research has found that as page load time goes from one second to three seconds, the probability of a mobile visitor bouncing increases by around 32%.

**Fix:** Compress images, remove unnecessary scripts and embeds, and test your page on a real phone with mobile data, not just wifi in the office.

## On the page: the visitor arrived but didn't act

### 4. Unclear value proposition
If a visitor has to scroll and think to work out what you're actually offering, you've already lost most of them. This is common on service business pages written for people who already understand the industry jargon.

**Fix:** State what you do, who it's for, and what happens next, in the first screen, in plain language. A reader should know within five seconds whether this is relevant to them.

### 5. No trust signals near the decision point
Price and action without proof is a hard ask, especially for a first-time buyer or a business making a B2B purchase decision. A visitor weighing up whether to hand over their card details or book a call wants some evidence this isn't a risk.

**Fix:** Put reviews, logos, case studies, or guarantees close to the CTA, not buried in a footer nobody scrolls to. For ecommerce, star ratings near the add-to-cart button do real work. For service businesses, a client logo or a specific result does the same job.

### 6. Buried or unclear call to action
"Learn More" tells a visitor nothing about what happens if they click. A CTA below three scrolls of content means most visitors never see it at all.

**Fix:** Use a specific, action-based CTA ("Get My Quote," "Add to Cart," "Book a Free Call") and repeat it at natural points down the page, not just once at the very top.

## In the form: they started but didn't finish

### 7. Asking for too much too soon
Every extra form field is a reason to abandon. A lead gen form asking for phone number, company size, budget, and timeline before the visitor has even decided you're worth talking to will lose people who would have converted with a lighter ask.

**Fix:** Ask for the minimum needed to start the conversation. Name and email, or name and phone, is often enough. Qualify further on the call or in a follow-up, not on the form.

### 8. Competing links and distractions
A landing page with full site navigation, a blog link, and three different offers in the sidebar gives the visitor an exit ramp from the one thing you actually want them to do.

**Fix:** Strip the navigation. A dedicated landing page should have one job and one path to completing it. Every link that isn't the CTA is a chance for the visitor to leave and never come back.

### 9. Broken or clunky mobile experience
Most traffic to most landing pages is mobile. A form that's fiddly to fill in on a small screen, a button that's hard to tap accurately, or text that requires pinching to read will cost you conversions that never show up as an obvious "bug."

**Fix:** Test the actual form completion on a phone, not just how the page looks. Large tap targets, auto-formatting for phone numbers, and minimal typing all measurably lift completion rates.

## How to find out which one is yours

Don't guess. Look at your analytics for where visitors drop: if they bounce immediately, suspect message mismatch, load time, or unclear value. If they scroll but don't click the CTA, suspect trust signals or CTA placement. If they start the form but abandon it, suspect field count or mobile friction.

Fix one thing at a time and watch the before-and-after. Changing five elements at once means you'll never know which one actually moved the number, and you'll be back here in six months with the same problem in a different disguise.

A landing page is one of the few parts of your funnel you control completely. No algorithm, no platform update, no competitor can touch it. That makes it worth getting right before you spend another dollar sending traffic to it.
