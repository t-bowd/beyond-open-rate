Melbourne has a strong e-commerce base and a growing professional services and SaaS sector, and both tend to underinvest in email relative to what the channel can return. The pattern is consistent: a list that's been built, a platform that's been set up, and email that's doing less than it should because the automation was never built correctly or the reporting never showed what was actually working.

That's the situation Beyond Open Rate typically walks into. Audit first, then fix or build from there.

## What email marketing services should do

The purpose of an email program is to generate consistent, compounding revenue from the list you've already built. For e-commerce, that means automated flows covering the full customer journey — welcome, abandoned browse and cart, post-purchase, replenishment, win-back — plus campaigns that drive seasonal and promotional revenue on top. For services businesses, it means nurture sequences, client retention programs, and re-engagement campaigns built around how clients actually make decisions.

Campaigns get the attention. Flows generate the revenue. Well-built automated sequences typically account for 25–35% of total email-attributed revenue despite representing a fraction of total sends.

## The audit-first process

Beyond Open Rate audits every account before recommending a single change. That means reviewing existing flows for logic and timing errors, checking deliverability and authentication setup, assessing segmentation quality, and identifying where the revenue is being lost. Most Melbourne businesses that have had email for more than a year have three to five fixable issues that are quietly limiting performance — the audit surfaces them in order of revenue impact.

## Platform independence

Beyond Open Rate works across Klaviyo, ActiveCampaign, HubSpot, Omnisend, and Brevo, and takes no referral fees from any of them. The platform recommendation, where one is needed, is based on the business's data model, revenue structure, and existing tech stack — not on which platform pays the highest commission.

## Reporting that means something

Monthly reporting covers revenue per recipient, contribution to total revenue, and flow-level performance. Not open rates, not click-through rates — the numbers that show whether the program is actually working.
