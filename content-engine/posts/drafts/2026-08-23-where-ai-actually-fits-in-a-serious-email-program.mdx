## Variant A

Let me guess what you asked AI to do last week.

Write a subject line. Fine.
Draft some product copy. Fine.
Decide which subscribers to suppress before your Black Friday send.

That last one should worry you.

Here's the split that matters.

AI is good at production. It is not good at judgment.

Use it for first drafts of your campaign copy. Use it for subject line variants when you're testing five angles instead of one. Use it to rephrase a product description for a winback email versus a browse abandonment email, same product, different context, different tone. Use it to spit out A/B test ideas when you've run out of your own.

That's the speed column. All of that is safe to hand over.

Now the outcomes column.

Deciding who gets suppressed from a send isn't a copywriting task. It's a judgment call based on engagement history, complaint risk, and what happens to your sender reputation if you get it wrong.

Setting flow trigger timing isn't guesswork either. It's based on your specific customer behaviour, your specific purchase cycle, and what you've learned from testing.

Identifying list hygiene problems requires knowing what "normal" looks like for your list. AI doesn't know your normal.

And sender reputation. If that gets damaged, every email you send from that point earns less, for everyone, not just the one campaign you were optimising.

One column saves you hours.
The other column, handled badly, costs you deliverability you don't get back quickly.

## Variant B

AI has made email production faster than it's ever been.

That part's true.

Drafts that took an hour now take four minutes.
Subject line testing that used to mean staring at a blank doc now means picking the best of fifteen options.
Product descriptions rewritten for six different flows in the time it used to take to write one.

All genuinely useful. All things we use ourselves.

Here's where it stops being useful.

The moment you ask AI to decide something instead of draft something.

Who gets suppressed from your next campaign. That's not a drafting task, that's a risk decision based on engagement data and complaint history.

When your abandoned cart flow should actually send. That's not a template, that's a decision built on your specific customers and how they actually behave, not how customers behave in general.

Whether your list has a hygiene problem building underneath your open rate. That takes knowing what your normal looks like over time, not pattern-matching against a generic dataset.

Whether your sender reputation is under pressure right now. Get this one wrong and you don't find out for weeks, by which point it's already cost you inbox placement.

Production tasks: hand them over, save the hours.
Outcome tasks: keep a human who understands your list making the call.

The brands getting this wrong aren't lazy. They just haven't drawn the line yet.
