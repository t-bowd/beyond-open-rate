## Variant A

The AI features in your email platform are probably already on.

Send time optimisation. Predictive scoring. Content recommendations. Some platforms now have AI making path decisions inside your flows — which sequence a contact goes into, what they see next, when they get pulled out.

This stuff is real and it's useful.

The brands getting results from it have one thing in common. The foundation was right before they turned it on.

Clean list. Engaged segments. Flows built around how their customers actually behave. Authentication solid. Suppression logic working. A clear picture of which contacts are drifting before they churn.

When that's in place, AI optimisation makes a good program better. It finds timing patterns you'd never spot manually. It adapts faster than any human running the flows could. It makes personalisation at scale actually mean something.

When it's not in place, AI optimisation makes the problems more efficient.

A suppression gap gets amplified. A poorly timed trigger reaches more people faster. A flow built on the wrong assumptions gets optimised in the wrong direction, which is harder to notice than just getting it wrong.

The question isn't whether to use AI in your email program. You probably already are, whether you know it or not.

The question is whether your program is set up well enough for AI to be working for you rather than against you.

Most aren't yet. That's the work.

Follow along or send a DM if you want to talk through what that looks like.

## Variant B

Three things AI can genuinely improve in your email automations right now.

And one thing that has to be in place before any of them work properly.

The three things.

Send timing. AI can find when your specific subscribers are most likely to open — not when people in general open email, but your list, your customers, their actual behaviour. For high-volume sends this compounds quickly.

Path decisions. The better platforms now let AI decide which sequence a contact goes into based on their real-time behaviour. That's not set-it-and-forget-it automation anymore. That's a program that responds.

Content relevance. AI personalisation that goes beyond first name and a product recommendation block. The right message for where someone actually is in their lifecycle, based on what they've done, not just a segment they got sorted into six months ago.

All of this exists. All of it is useful.

The one thing that has to be in place first.

A list clean enough and a program structured well enough for the AI to be optimising in the right direction.

Send time optimisation on a disengaged list just delivers noise more efficiently. Path decisions built on the wrong trigger logic move people through the wrong journey faster. Content personalisation without decent behavioural data is pattern-matching on thin signal.

The ceiling for AI in your email program is your foundation.
