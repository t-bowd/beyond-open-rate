## Variant A

Braze shipped Agent Steps last week.

An AI layer inside the platform that makes decisions about your flows — which path a contact takes, when to send, what content to show — without a human in the loop.

That's not a future feature. It's live now.

And it's not just Braze. Every major platform is moving this direction. AI that doesn't just draft copy but actively runs your program.

Here's the thing though.

The AI is only as good as what it's working from.

Agent Steps can decide which path to take. It can't decide which paths should exist in the first place. It can optimise send time across your list. It can't tell you whether your list is clean enough for that optimisation to mean anything. It can personalise content based on behaviour. It can't define what behaviour signals actually matter for your specific customer.

The strategy still has to come from somewhere.

Which segments you build. What triggers are worth building flows around. Whether your deliverability is solid enough to let AI optimise on top of it rather than compound a problem you haven't noticed yet. How to read what the AI is actually doing and whether it's making the right call for your business.

That's the part that still needs someone who understands your program.

The platforms are getting smarter. What they're not doing is replacing the strategy that makes them work.

## Variant B

The platforms have caught up faster than most people expected.

Braze has AI agent steps now. Send time optimisation has been table stakes for two years. Content recommendations, predictive scoring, AI-generated copy — it's all inside the tools already.

So the question isn't whether AI can make decisions in your email program. It can. The question is whether it's making the right decisions for your specific business.

And that depends entirely on what's underneath it.

An AI making suppression decisions needs to understand your list. Not lists in general — your list, your engagement baseline, what churn looks like for your customer type.

An AI optimising send time needs a clean, healthy list to optimise on. If deliverability is already under pressure, optimising on top of that makes it worse faster.

An AI personalising content needs the right flows built underneath it. If the architecture is wrong, smarter content at each step just moves people through the wrong journey more efficiently.

The platforms are good. Better than they've ever been. But they're amplifiers. They make a well-built program perform better. They make a poorly built program fail more confidently.

What we do is build the foundation the AI runs on.

Every brand that gets serious about AI-driven email still needs someone to build the strategy it executes.
