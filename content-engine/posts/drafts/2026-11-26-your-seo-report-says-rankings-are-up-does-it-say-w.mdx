## Variant A

Your SEO report landed this morning.

Rankings up. Impressions up. A nice green arrow next to both.

You feel good for about four minutes.

Then you check the phone. Check the inbox. Check last month's sales.

Quiet.

Here's the thing nobody tells you when you sign up for SEO. Rankings and impressions measure whether Google likes you. They don't measure whether customers are paying you.

You can rank for a keyword nobody searches with intent to buy. You can get thousands of impressions from people who scroll straight past. Both of those show up as wins on a standard report.

Neither one pays your rent.

For an online store, the number that matters is orders from organic traffic, and what those orders are worth over time. Not sessions. Not keyword position.

For a service business, it's enquiries that turn into booked calls. Not "we're now ranking page one for twelve terms you've never heard a client say out loud."

A report full of green arrows can sit next to a business that's going backwards. It happens more than most agencies will admit, because rankings are easy to show and revenue is harder to attribute properly.

If your SEO reporting stops at position and traffic, you're trusting that the rest sorts itself out.

Sometimes it does. Often it doesn't.

We report on SEO the same way we report on everything else. What it earned, not what it ranked.

## Variant B

Rankings are the lever everyone watches.

Position three for this term. Position one for that one. Up seven places since last quarter.

It feels like the right thing to track because it's the thing that's visible and it's the thing most SEO reports are built around.

But rankings are not the lever that pays your bills. Enquiries and sales are.

Picture two reports for the same business, same month.

Report one: rankings up across the board, impressions climbing, traffic trending the right way. Looks like a strong month.

Report two: four enquiries from organic search, one of them turned into a client worth real money over the next year.

Same underlying data. Completely different story depending on which one you're handed.

Most agencies hand you report one, because it's easier to produce and always looks good. Rankings almost always go up somewhere if you wait long enough. Revenue from those rankings is a different question entirely, and it's the one that actually tells you whether to keep paying.

For an online store, that means tracking which organic pages lead to purchases, not just which ones get clicks.

For a service business, it means knowing which search terms bring in people who book a call, not just people who land on the page and bounce.

If your current reporting stops at rankings, you're only seeing half the picture, and it's the half that doesn't pay you.
