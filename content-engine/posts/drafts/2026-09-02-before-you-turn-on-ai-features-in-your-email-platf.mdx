## Variant A

You turned on send time optimisation last week.

Maybe predictive scoring too.

Felt like a good move. Everyone's doing it.

Here's the uncomfortable part.

AI doesn't know if your list is healthy.
It doesn't know if your flows were built for your business or copied from a template.
It doesn't know if your data is thick enough to learn from.

It just optimises.

Based on whatever it's given.

If what it's given is thin, generic, or messy, it will still produce an answer. Confidently. And it'll look like it's working, because you'll get a report with a percentage on it.

That's the trap.

AI doesn't fail loudly. It fails quietly, by learning the wrong pattern and reinforcing it every send.

Before you trust the output, check five things:

List hygiene. Do you have a real engagement baseline, and is suppression actually removing dead contacts, or just labelling them?

Flow architecture. Are your triggers specific to how your customers actually behave, or are they the same three flows every template ships with?

Data quality. Is there enough behavioural signal (clicks, purchases, site activity) for the model to learn from, or is it working off scraps?

Authentication and deliverability. If your emails aren't landing in the inbox reliably, AI is optimising a channel that's already broken.

Reporting. Are you measuring revenue per send, or still celebrating open rate?

Get these five wrong and AI won't fix your email program.

It'll just make the wrong decisions faster.

## Variant B

AI send time optimisation doesn't know your customers.

It knows your data.

Predictive scoring, path decisions, next-best-send. All of it runs on what you feed it. Your list, your flows, your history.

Feed it a clean, well-behaved list and it'll make sharp calls.

Feed it a list full of dead contacts, generic flows, and thin behavioural data, and it'll still make calls.

Just wrong ones.

Confidently.

Before you turn any of it on, or if you already have, run through this.

List hygiene. Check your engagement baseline. Confirm suppression is actually pulling unengaged contacts out, not just flagging them and leaving them in the send.

Flow architecture. Generic templates give AI generic signal to learn from. If your welcome series and abandon flows aren't built around how this specific business sells, the model has nothing real to optimise.

Data quality. AI needs volume and variety of behavioural signal, clicks, purchases, browsing, to make good predictions. A small or shallow dataset gives it almost nothing to work with.

Authentication and deliverability. If emails aren't landing in the inbox consistently, you're asking AI to optimise a channel that's already underperforming at the foundation.

Reporting. Check what you're actually measuring. Open rate tells you nothing about revenue. Recovered cart value and repeat purchase rate do.

Miss any one of these and the AI isn't broken.

It's just optimising in the wrong direction, and doing it without any obvious warning sign.
