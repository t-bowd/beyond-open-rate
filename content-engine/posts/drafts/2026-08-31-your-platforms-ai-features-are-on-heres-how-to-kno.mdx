## Variant A

You turned on send time optimisation.

You turned on predictive scoring.

You let the platform pick the winning path in your flows.

Then you moved on with your life.

Fair enough. That's what the features are for.

But most people never go back and check if any of it is actually doing something.

Here's the problem. These tools don't fail loudly. They fail quietly, by getting very efficient at optimising a list that was never that engaged to begin with. The dashboard still looks busy. The number that matters doesn't move.

Three things worth checking this week.

Send time optimisation. Don't look at open rate, look at reply-to-open ratio. If opens are shifting but replies and clicks aren't improving, the tool is just moving the same result to a different hour.

Predictive scoring. Pull the top 200 contacts it's flagged as "high intent." Now pull your top 200 by simple recency and frequency of engagement. If it's mostly the same names, the AI isn't finding anything a basic segment wouldn't have found for free.

AI path variation in flows. Check if the AI-selected path is actually beating a fixed control over a real sample size, not just beating "no email at all." Winning against nothing isn't a win.

None of this means turn the features off.

It means don't assume "on" means "working."

## Variant B

Predictive scoring can't fix a disengaged list. It can only get better at working within one.

That's the part most brands miss when they flip these features on and stop thinking about email.

If your list has been quietly decaying for a year, and you turn on AI send time optimisation, here's what actually happens. The tool finds the hour each contact is statistically most likely to open. Opens might even tick up. But if those contacts were never going to buy anything, you've just optimised the timing of a non-event.

Same with predictive lead scoring. It's built to rank contacts by likelihood to convert, based on behaviour it can see. If your engaged segment is small and your disengaged segment is huge, it will often just reconstruct what a basic "opened or clicked in the last 60 days" segment already told you, dressed up as intelligence.

And AI path selection in flows. Worth asking whether the path it picked is actually outperforming a fixed control you set manually, tested properly, over a decent sample size. Not outperforming silence. Outperforming your best guess.

Three checks, five minutes each.

Compare reply-to-open ratio before and after send time optimisation, not just open rate.

Compare your AI-flagged high-intent list against a simple recency and frequency segment.

Compare AI-selected flow paths against a manual control over enough sends to mean something.

If the answers surprise you, the tools aren't broken. The list underneath them probably needs attention first.
