## Variant A

Let me guess what you did after the ad stopped converting.

You changed the headline.

Then the image.

Then the headline again.

Three versions later, same result.

Here's what actually happened when someone clicked.

They land on a page that still talks about something slightly different to what the ad promised.

There's no proof they're in the right place.

The button is below three paragraphs they'll never read.

On mobile, half of it doesn't fit the screen properly.

They're gone in four seconds. Not because the ad was weak. Because the page gave them nothing to do with their attention.

If you're running an online store, this is the visitor who was ready to buy and bounced because the page didn't match the offer that got them there.

If you run a service business, this is the person who clicked because your ad solved their exact problem, then landed on a generic "About Us" page with a contact form buried at the bottom.

The ad's only job is to get the click.

The page's job is to turn that click into money.

You can have the best ad in your industry and still lose almost everyone, because nobody fixes the part that actually decides whether the spend was worth it.

Next time a campaign underperforms, open the page it sends people to before you touch the ad.

## Variant B

Someone sees your ad.

It's good. It stops the scroll. It says exactly the right thing to the right person.

They click.

Now watch what happens next.

The page loads a second slower than it should.

The headline on the page doesn't match what the ad just promised them.

They scroll, looking for the thing that convinced them to click. It's not there, or it's buried under three sections of copy about your company history.

They're still interested. They just can't find the answer fast enough.

So they leave.

You paid for that click whether they stayed four seconds or forty.

Most founders watch this happen and conclude the ad underperformed. So they write a new one. Test a new image. Try a different hook.

The ad keeps changing. The page never does.

For a service business, that page is where someone decides whether to book a call or close the tab.

For an online store, it's where someone decides whether to add to cart or go compare you on a search engine.

Either way, the ad only earns you the chance. The page decides if you get paid for it.

If your ads keep "underperforming" and the creative keeps changing, look at where the money actually falls over.
