## Variant A

You check your repeat purchase rate every month.

Good habit.

Here's the problem with it.

By the time a customer shows up in that number, they've already lapsed.

You're measuring the funeral, not the illness.

The customer didn't go quiet overnight. There were signals for weeks, maybe months, before they stopped buying.

Their engagement tier dropped from "regular opener" to "occasional." Their RFM score (a simple way of ranking customers by how recently, how often, and how much they spend) started sliding down instead of up.

The gap between their last two orders got longer than the average for your category.

They started browsing product pages and adding nothing to cart.

Every one of those is a signal sitting in your email data right now.

Most brands aren't looking for it. They're using AI to write faster subject lines and better copy.

That's the small use case.

The bigger one is asking AI to read those signals across your whole list and flag exactly who's drifting, before they're gone.

Because here's the maths that matters. Retaining a customer who's starting to slip costs a fraction of acquiring a new one to replace them.

You already paid to get them once.

The cheapest revenue you'll ever generate is the customer you almost lost and caught in time.

Most brands find out who that was after the fact. A few find out while there's still something to do about it.

## Variant B

Ask most founders how they're using AI in email and you'll get the same answer.

Writing subject lines. Drafting campaigns. Tightening up copy.

Useful. Not where the money is.

The higher-value use of AI in email isn't writing the message. It's telling you who urgently needs to receive it.

Every customer who's about to lapse leaves a trail before they go quiet.

Their purchase interval stretches longer than the category average. Their engagement tier slips, from opening every campaign to skipping most of them. Their RFM score starts moving in the wrong direction. They browse the same product three times and never buy.

None of that shows up in your repeat purchase rate.

That number only tells you who's already gone.

AI can read those signals across your entire list and rank who's drifting right now, while there's still time to do something about it.

Most brands skip this step entirely. They wait for the repeat purchase number to drop, then scramble to figure out why.

The businesses that retain more customers aren't better at winning them back.

They're better at noticing before they leave.

Worth remembering going into next year's planning: keeping an existing customer is almost always cheaper than replacing one you lost without noticing.
