## Variant A

Rankings up 14 positions.

Impressions up 30%.

You open the report, see the green arrows, feel better about the invoice.

Then you check the phone.

Nothing.

Check the enquiry form.

Last submission was three weeks ago.

This is the part nobody explains when they sell you SEO. Rankings are a proxy. Impressions are a proxy. They measure whether Google likes you, not whether customers do.

You can rank first for a term nobody searches with intent to buy. You can get thousands of impressions from people who scroll straight past. Both of those will make a report look brilliant.

Neither one pays your rent.

For an ecommerce brand, the number that matters is organic revenue and how many of those sessions turn into orders. For a service business, it's enquiries that turn into booked calls, and calls that turn into clients.

If your report doesn't connect rankings to one of those two things, you're not being told whether SEO is working. You're being told whether it looks like it's working.

Ask whoever runs your SEO to show you the line from keyword to click to enquiry to sale. If they can't draw that line, the rankings are decoration.

We report it that way for every client, because a green arrow that doesn't lead to a sale isn't a result. It's a screenshot.

## Variant B

There are two kinds of SEO reports.

One tells you what Google thinks of your website.

The other tells you what your website earned.

Most business owners only ever see the first one.

Rankings. Impressions. Click-through rate. Traffic graphs trending up and to the right. All real numbers. All easy to screenshot. None of them tell you whether a single person bought anything or picked up the phone.

Here's the gap in plain terms. Say a keyword moves from position 9 to position 3. Impressions jump. Traffic to that page doubles. That's the story most reports stop at, and it sounds like a win.

But if that page is the wrong page, or the offer on it doesn't match what the searcher wanted, all you've done is get more of the wrong people to leave empty-handed. The ranking went up. The revenue didn't move.

For an ecommerce store, the number to chase is organic sessions that convert to orders, by page, by product category. For a service business, it's organic enquiries that turn into booked calls, and which search terms actually produced a client.

Rankings are an input. Revenue is the output. A good SEO report shows you both and draws the line between them.

If yours only shows the first half, you're not seeing whether it's working. You're seeing whether it looks like it's working.

That's the standard we hold our own SEO reporting to, and happy to show you what that looks like if you're curious.
