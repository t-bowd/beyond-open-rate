## Variant A

You posted three times this week.

Checked the likes each time.

Felt good when the number went up. Felt a bit flat when it didn't.

Now try this.

Open your bank account.

Find the line where social media turned into money.

You can't. Because that number doesn't exist in your account. It only exists on the platform, and the platform built it to keep you scrolling and posting, not to make you richer.

Likes are a mood ring. Reach is a vanity metric with a nice chart. Neither one has ever paid an invoice.

Here's what actually tells you if social is working.

Enquiries that mention your content. Someone messages, books a call, or fills out a form and references a post they saw.

Branded searches. People typing your business name into a search bar after seeing you online, instead of scrolling past.

Customers who say it out loud. "I saw you on Instagram" at checkout, on a call, in a review.

None of these need a dashboard. A notes app and a habit of asking "where did you hear about us" will do it.

Track those three for a month.

Then decide if the content strategy is working or if it's just keeping you busy.

## Variant B

Posting consistently is not a marketing strategy. It's a habit.

You can post every day for a year and still not know if it earned you a single dollar.

That's not a dig. It's just how the numbers work.

Likes measure who stopped scrolling for half a second. Reach measures how many people the platform showed your post to. Both numbers go up and down for reasons that have nothing to do with your business. An algorithm change, a trending sound, a Tuesday. None of it is revenue.

So stop measuring the mood of the post and start measuring the money.

Three things. No fancy tools.

One. Enquiries that reference social. When someone contacts you, ask how they found you. Write it down. If "Instagram" or "LinkedIn" keeps coming up, that's signal.

Two. Branded search volume. Are more people typing your business name into a search bar than six months ago? That's curiosity turning into intent.

Three. The verbal mention. "I've been following you for a while" before someone buys or books. That line is worth more than a thousand likes, because it means the content did its job before the sale even started.

Likes tell you the post was seen.

These three tell you the post was worth making.
