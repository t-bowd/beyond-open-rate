## Variant A

You open the report.

Rankings up.

Impressions up.

Clicks trending the right way.

You close the laptop feeling like SEO is finally working.

Then you check the phone.

Same number of enquiries as last month.

Same number of orders.

Nothing moved.

Here's the part most business owners never get told. Ranking higher for a keyword doesn't mean anything on its own. You can rank number one for a term nobody searches with money in hand. You can get more impressions because Google is now showing your page to people browsing, not buying.

A report full of green arrows can sit next to a quiet phone and a quiet inbox for months.

That's not a glitch. That's what happens when SEO gets reported on activity instead of outcome.

Positions are a leading indicator. They're useful, but they're not the point. The point is whether that ranking turned into a form filled out, a call booked, a product added to cart.

Good SEO reporting answers one question. Did this traffic turn into revenue, and if not, where did it stall. On the page. In the form. In the follow up.

If your reports can't answer that, you're not being told whether SEO is working. You're being told whether SEO is busy.

Worth asking your provider to show both. Rankings, and what the rankings actually earned.

## Variant B

Your SEO report can look perfect and still be failing your business.

Rankings climbing. Impressions growing. Traffic up on last quarter.

None of that pays the rent.

Here's the gap nobody explains when you sign up for SEO. Rankings measure visibility. Visibility is not revenue. It's the step before revenue, and plenty of businesses get stuck right there, paying monthly for a number that goes up while the phone stays exactly as quiet as it was six months ago.

Two businesses can have identical ranking reports. One is booking calls every week. The other hasn't had a new enquiry in a month. Same positions. Same impressions. Completely different outcomes.

The difference is what happens after someone clicks. Does the page answer the question they searched. Does it make the next step obvious. Does anyone follow up when they fill out the form.

None of that shows up in a rankings report. All of it shows up in a revenue report.

If you're paying for SEO, you're entitled to know two things every month. Where you rank, and what that ranking earned. Enquiries. Bookings. Orders. Not just the climb, the catch.

A rankings report tells you SEO is active.

A revenue report tells you SEO is working.

Most businesses are only ever shown the first one.
