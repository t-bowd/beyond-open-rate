## Variant A

Open five browser tabs.

Five different brands. Five different industries.

Pull up their last email campaign.

Read the subject lines. Read the first line of body copy. Read the sign-off.

They sound the same.

Same cadence. Short punchy line, then a slightly longer one explaining it.

Same faux-casual tone. Like a founder texting you, except no founder actually talks like that.

Same structure every time. Hook, mini story, pivot to offer, urgency line, button.

That's not a coincidence.

That's every brand feeding the same prompt into the same tool and getting the same output back.

You've probably done it too. Drafted a campaign in seconds. Tweaked a few words. Hit send.

It reads fine. It's grammatically correct. It's also completely forgettable.

Brand voice used to be something you built slowly, over hundreds of emails, hundreds of small decisions about how you sound different to the brand next to you.

Now most brands have handed that decision to a tool that's trained on everyone else's emails too.

Which means the brands still writing in a way that actually sounds like them are becoming easier to spot.

Not because they're doing something clever.

Because everyone else stopped bothering to sound like anyone in particular.

## Variant B

Something happened to email in the last two years that most brands haven't noticed yet.

The writing got better and worse at the same time.

Better, because almost nobody sends a typo-ridden, badly structured email anymore. AI cleaned that up.

Worse, because almost every brand is now running that email through the same handful of tools, trained on the same data, producing the same patterns.

The same rhythm. Short line. Line that explains it. Casual aside in brackets.

The same structural beats every single time.

If you've been using AI to draft your campaigns, you're not doing anything wrong. It's faster. It's cheaper than a copywriter. It gets a decent email out the door in minutes instead of hours.

But here's what it's doing underneath that you probably haven't priced in.

It's making your brand sound like every other brand using the same tool.

Brand voice used to be hard to hold onto at scale. You'd hire writers, brief them for hours, still end up with inconsistency by email forty.

Now it's not hard to hold onto. It's just rare that anyone bothers.

Which means a distinctive voice in the inbox isn't a nice-to-have anymore.

It's one of the only things left that AI can't standardise for you, unless you let it.
