## Variant A

Your emails say "Hi Sarah."

They show Sarah products based on what she last browsed.

You call this personalisation.

It isn't nothing. But it's the easy 80%.

Here's the harder 20%, the part your customer engagement platform doesn't do for you.

Sarah bought three times in four months, then went quiet. That's not random. That's a lapsing customer, and there's a window before she's gone for good.

Meanwhile Jake has bought three times too, but every single time, and each order was bigger than the last. Jake isn't a candidate for 10% off. Jake is ready for your $400 offer, and a discount would insult him.

And Priya just returned the jacket she bought two weeks ago. If your flow puts her straight into "customers who bought this also bought," you've just told her to buy something else from the brand that already let her down once.

Three customers. Three completely different situations. Same merge tag, same recommendation block, same generic email.

The tools can personalise the surface. Name, product, maybe the send time.

They can't tell you who's about to leave, who's ready to spend more, or who needs silence instead of a sales pitch.

That's not a feature you install. That's a decision someone has to make about your list, your customers, your business.

Most brands have the first kind of personalisation and think that's the whole job.

It's the part that was always going to be easy.

## Variant B

Three customers open your email this morning.

One bought three times in the last four months and hasn't opened an email in three weeks.

One bought three times in the last four months and just placed her biggest order yet.

One returned a product ten days ago and is still waiting on the refund.

Your email platform sees three loyal customers. Sends all three the same "here's what you'll love" block, powered by whatever they clicked on last.

That's the recommendation engine doing its job. It's good at that job.

But it has no idea one of them is about to churn.

It has no idea one of them just proved she'll spend more if you ask properly, instead of showing her the same $40 item everyone else gets.

It has no idea the third one is currently annoyed at you, and a cross-sell right now reads as tone-deaf, not helpful.

Merge tags and AI-generated product blocks are personalisation at the level of "what do you already like." Useful. Fast. Table stakes now.

The kind that actually changes revenue is personalisation at the level of "what does this specific customer need from us right now."

Who's drifting.
Who's ready for more.
Who just got burned and needs to be left alone for a fortnight.

No tool ships with that built in. It comes from someone deciding, deliberately, how those three customers should be treated differently.

That decision is the part most brands skip.
