## Variant A

Your platform has AI personalisation built in.

You probably turned it on eighteen months ago.

Send time optimisation. Maybe a subject line generator. Some kind of "smart" toggle you switched on and never thought about again.

Here's the problem.

That's the easy level. It works with almost no setup. It's also the least valuable part of what's possible.

There are three levels here, and most brands are stuck on level one.

Level one: send time. The AI works out when an individual is most likely to open, and sends then instead of your fixed schedule. Easy to turn on. Small lift. No data connection required.

Level two: content blocks. The AI shows different products or categories inside the same email based on what someone actually browsed or bought. This needs your product and category data connected properly. Most brands haven't done this, so the AI has nothing to work with. It defaults to generic.

Level three: path decisions. The AI decides which flow someone should be in, or which message they should get next, based on lifecycle stage and behaviour. A browser who's been quiet for 40 days gets treated differently to someone who bought last week. This needs clean behavioural data and flows built specifically to use it.

Most brands are sitting on level one and assuming that's "AI personalisation."

The AI was never the bottleneck. The data feeding it is.

We spend most of our time connecting that data and building flows that actually use levels two and three, because that's where the revenue difference shows up.

## Variant B

Every major email platform now says "powered by AI."

Here's what that actually means, and where it breaks down.

AI personalisation isn't one thing. It's three, and they need different inputs.

Send time is the simplest. The AI looks at when an individual has opened in the past and sends to that window instead of blasting everyone at 9am. No extra data needed. If you haven't turned this on, it's a five minute fix with no downside.

Content blocks are the next level. Different subscribers see different products in the same email, based on what they browsed or bought. This is where most brands stall, because the AI can only personalise with information it's been given. If your product and category data isn't connected properly, the AI has nothing to work with and falls back to generic content. Looks personalised. Isn't.

Path decisions are the advanced level. The AI decides whether someone gets a nurture email or a win-back email, based on where they sit in the lifecycle and how long since they last engaged. This needs clean behavioural data and flows built to actually use it, not just a template with an AI label slapped on top.

Most brands buy a platform because it says AI. Then wonder why personalisation still feels shallow.

The AI was never missing. The connected data was.

That's the part we build before we touch a single flow.
