## Variant A

You turned on send time optimisation.

You turned on predictive scoring.

You let the platform pick the winning path in your flows.

Then you moved on to the next thing.

That was eighteen months ago.

Nobody's checked since.

Here's the problem. These features don't fail loudly. They don't send you an alert saying "this isn't working." They just keep running, quietly making decisions on a list that might be in worse shape than it was when you switched them on.

So here's what to actually check.

Send time optimisation. It's supposed to send emails when someone is most likely to engage. Check whether it's improving your reply-to-open ratio, not just moving opens around the clock. If people are opening at 7am instead of 2pm but still not clicking or replying, the AI shifted timing. It didn't fix engagement.

Predictive scoring. It's meant to surface your best contacts. Pull that list. Now pull a basic segment of anyone who's opened or clicked in the last 60 days. If the two lists look almost identical, the AI isn't adding insight. It's repackaging what you already knew.

AI path decisions in flows. Most platforms let AI choose which version of an email a contact sees. Find the control group. Compare revenue per recipient, not open rate. If the AI path isn't beating a plain, un-optimised version, you're paying a complexity tax for no return.

None of this means turn the features off.

It means stop assuming "on" equals "working."

## Variant B

Predictive scoring, AI send times, automated path testing.

Most platforms have these built in now.

Most brands flip them on and assume the job is done.

Here's what nobody tells you when you turn them on.

AI optimises whatever it's given. It doesn't know if your list is healthy or half dead. It just works with what's there.

So if your list has been quietly decaying for a year, the AI isn't fixing that. It's finding the most efficient way to talk to a shrinking pool of engaged contacts while the disengaged majority sits there, unaddressed, looking fine on a dashboard because the metrics that matter are trending the wrong way slowly enough to miss.

Three things worth checking this week.

Pull your predictive "best contacts" list. Compare it to a simple segment of anyone who's opened or clicked in the last 60 days. If they're basically the same people, the AI isn't finding anything a manual segment couldn't.

Check your send time optimisation. Opens might be shifting to different hours without any lift in replies, clicks, or purchases. Moved timing isn't improved performance.

Look at your AI-selected path in any automated flow. Compare it against a fixed control, same audience, same offer. If it's not outperforming on revenue per recipient, the feature is adding noise, not value.

The tools aren't broken. But they'll happily optimise a shrinking, disengaged list without ever telling you that's what's happening.
