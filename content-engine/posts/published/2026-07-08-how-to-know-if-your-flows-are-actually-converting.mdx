## Variant A

You're looking at your email dashboard.

Welcome series: 42% open rate. Nice.
Cart abandonment: 18% click rate. Great.
Win-back: 31% engagement. Solid.

But here's the problem.

None of those numbers tell you if the flow made money.

Opens mean someone glanced at the subject line. Clicks mean curiosity. Engagement means literally nothing unless it's tied to a purchase.

The only number that matters is revenue generated.

How much did that welcome series drive in first purchase value? How much cart revenue did you actually recover? What's the incremental lift from that win-back sequence?

If your reporting doesn't answer those questions, you're measuring activity. Not outcomes.

Every flow should have a dollar figure attached to it. If it doesn't, you're flying blind.

Start asking for revenue data on every flow you run.

## Variant B

You spent six hours setting up that cart abandonment flow.

You wrote the copy. Built the sequence. Set the delays. Nailed the subject lines.

Two weeks later, you check the dashboard.

"15% open rate. 4% click rate. Looks fine."

But you have no idea if it made you money.

Most email platforms show you activity by default. Opens, clicks, unsubscribes. All surface-level. None of it tells you whether the flow did its job.

A high open rate on a cart recovery flow means nothing if it's only recovering 5% of potential revenue.

The metric you need is recovery rate. Or incremental revenue. Or LTV change.

Not clicks. Dollars.

If your flows don't have a revenue number next to them, you're not measuring what matters. You're just watching people move through a funnel and hoping it works.
