## Variant A

Everyone's asking whether AI wrote the email.

Nobody's asking whether AI is filtering it.

Mailbox providers have been running AI-driven filtering for years. Gmail. Outlook. Yahoo. The blocklist operators too.

The old filters checked keywords and authentication. Pass the rules, land in the inbox.

The new filters model probability. How likely is this message to be something this specific person wants? What does the sender's history say about content quality? Does this engagement pattern look like a healthy list or a cold one?

An email can pass every authentication check and still get filtered.

Even if it's not spam. Because the algorithm decided it wasn't worth delivering.

The signals hurting senders right now:

Large lists with low engagement. Every send to an unresponsive subscriber tells the algorithm your mail isn't wanted. That signal accumulates across every recipient at that provider.

Generic broadcasts sent to everyone. Per-recipient relevance modelling means a message that performs fine with one segment can be filtered aggressively for another.

Subject lines that overpromise. AI systems can detect mismatches between what an email claims and what it delivers. Consistently doing this builds a content quality problem over time.

The brands that understand this early have a structural advantage.

The ones that don't will keep wondering why their open rates are drifting.

## Variant B

Your authentication is clean.

SPF. DKIM. DMARC. All passing.

You're not on any blocklists.

But your open rates have been drifting for three months.

This is what AI filtering looks like.

It doesn't trip obvious alarms. It just quietly reduces your reach.

Mailbox providers aren't just checking whether you're a spammer. They're modelling whether each email is worth delivering to each specific recipient.

Engagement history. Content quality signals. Sending patterns. List health.

All of it feeds a reputation score that affects where your emails land across the entire provider's network.

The result: a brand with clean authentication but a disengaged list gets filtered the same way a spammer does. Just without the blocklist entry to point at.

What moves the score in the right direction:

Sunsetting subscribers who haven't engaged in 90 days. Continuing to send to them hurts reputation on every send.

Tighter segmentation. Sends with better relevance accumulate better engagement signals.

Consistent content quality. The gap between your subject line and your email body is something modern filters can read.

None of this is new advice. The difference is that now it's a deliverability issue, not just a marketing one.
