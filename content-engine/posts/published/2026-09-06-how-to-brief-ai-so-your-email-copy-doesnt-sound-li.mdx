## Variant A

You asked AI to write your abandoned cart email.

It came back fine.

Grammatically correct. On topic. Technically an email.

So why did you rewrite half of it?

Here's what's happening. AI is trained on the internet. The internet is full of email. Millions of brands, all sounding roughly the same, because they were all trained on each other.

Ask a generic question, get a generic answer. That's not an AI problem. That's a brief problem.

Most people give AI a one-line prompt and expect a finished brand voice. That's like handing a freelancer a topic and no context, then being surprised the copy needs a rewrite.

The brief is the craft now. Here's what a real one looks like.

The job. Not "write a cart abandonment email." Try "this email fires two hours after cart abandonment, for someone who's bought once before, and its only job is to get them back to checkout, not to build brand affection."

The segment. First-time browser versus repeat customer versus lapsed VIP. Completely different tone, completely different offer logic.

Three examples of copy that already sounds like you. Paste your best subject line, your best product description, an email you're proud of. AI mimics patterns. Give it a pattern worth mimicking.

What to avoid. Overused words, exclamation marks, false urgency, anything that sounds like a template.

The CTA and why it exists. "Complete your order" is not the same instruction as "remind them what's in the cart without sounding desperate."

Feed it that, and the draft needs a polish, not a rewrite.

## Variant B

Weak brief. Weak output. Every time.

Strong brief. Strong output. Every time.

Most people skip straight to "write me an email" and wonder why it reads like every other email in their inbox.

AI didn't fail you. The brief did.

Here's the difference, side by side.

Weak brief: "Write a win-back email for customers who haven't purchased in 90 days."

What you get back: generic urgency, a discount code, an exclamation mark you'll delete anyway. Sounds like every win-back email ever written, because it was trained on every win-back email ever written.

Strong brief looks like this.

Job: bring back customers who loved us once and drifted, without leading with a discount, because our margin can't absorb one more.

Segment: bought twice or more, nothing in 90 days, opened our last three emails but didn't click.

Voice examples: paste three lines of copy you'd actually put your name on. A subject line that got a big open rate. A product description your customers quote back to you. An email your best customer replied to.

Avoid: "we miss you," "exclusive offer," anything that reads like a template pulled from a swipe file.

CTA and reason: "see what's new" not "shop now," because this segment responds to curiosity, not pressure. You know that. AI doesn't, unless you tell it.

Same tool. Same model. Completely different output.

The prompt used to be the skill. Now the brief is.

The founders getting genuinely good AI copy aren't better writers. They're better briefers.
