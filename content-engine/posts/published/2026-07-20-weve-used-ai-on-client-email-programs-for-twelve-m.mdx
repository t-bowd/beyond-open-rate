## Variant A

Twelve months ago we started using AI across client email programs.

Not as an experiment. As part of the actual workflow.

Here's the honest split.

Good at:
First drafts of high-volume sequences. Post-purchase flows, browse abandonment, replenishment reminders.
Subject line variants for testing. Give it ten angles in thirty seconds.
Rephrasing product descriptions so they fit lifecycle context instead of reading like a product page.

Useless at:
Deciding who should actually receive a campaign. Segmentation is a judgment call, not a prompt.
Setting suppression windows. Send too soon after a purchase and you annoy a happy customer. Too late and you miss the reorder window. That call comes from knowing the customer, not the copy.
Spotting early deliverability decline. By the time the data shows it clearly, you've already lost inbox placement for two weeks.

The pattern is consistent. AI writes well when the inputs are clear.
It has no judgment when the inputs are messy, which is most of retention marketing.

We use it every day. We don't let it near the decisions that actually move revenue.

## Variant B

Last month AI wrote forty subject line variants for a client's Black Friday sequence in under a minute.

Three weeks before that, it would have quietly torched a different client's sender reputation if we'd let it decide the send schedule on its own.

Both are true. That's the point.

We've run AI across client email programs for twelve months now. Long enough to know exactly where the line sits.

It's genuinely fast and genuinely good at drafting high-volume sequences, generating subject line variants, and turning flat product descriptions into copy that fits a lifecycle moment.

It has no idea who should be suppressed from a send. No idea that a customer who just returned an order shouldn't get a upsell email tomorrow. No idea that a domain's engagement rate has been sliding for two weeks and is about to fall off a cliff.

Those calls need someone watching the account, not a model predicting the next likely word.

Twelve months in, our view hasn't changed much. AI is a fast drafting tool. It is not a strategist, and it definitely isn't a deliverability early warning system.
