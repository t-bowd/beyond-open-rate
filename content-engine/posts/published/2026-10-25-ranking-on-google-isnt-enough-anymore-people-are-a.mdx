## Variant A

You rank page one.

Took a while. Cost a bit. Worth it.

Someone searches your service right now though, and they don't open Google.

They open ChatGPT and ask who's good.

Your page one ranking means nothing in that conversation.

Different question. Different answer. Different business might get named.

This isn't a small shift. A growing number of buyers are asking an AI assistant who to hire before they ever type a search term in.

They want the answer handed to them, not ten blue links to sort through.

Getting named in that answer is not the same job as ranking a page.

Nobody can promise you'll be the one it recommends. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something.

But a few things are known.

AI assistants lean on businesses that have clear, consistent information about what they do and who they do it for, repeated the same way across the places that get crawled.

They lean on businesses that get mentioned elsewhere. Reviews, directories, press, other people's content. Not just your own website talking about yourself.

And they give a straight answer when a straight answer exists. Vague positioning doesn't get repeated. Specific positioning does.

If your last marketing conversation was entirely about keywords, it's worth having a different one.

## Variant B

Think about the last time you needed a tradesperson, a lawyer, an accountant you'd never used before.

Did you open Google and scroll.

Or did you ask ChatGPT who's good and take the first two names it gave you.

If it's the second one, you're not unusual. You're early, but not alone.

Now flip it around.

Someone is asking that same question about your industry, in your city, right now.

The AI assistant is giving them an answer.

Your business is either in it or it isn't.

Here's the uncomfortable part. Ranking well on Google doesn't guarantee you're in that answer. It's a different mechanism entirely. Google ranks pages. AI assistants generate a recommendation, built from whatever reliable information they can find about you across the web.

Most businesses that have invested properly in SEO have not touched this. Not because it's hard to understand, but because nobody told them it was a separate job.

Nobody can promise placement. The models change, the sources they trust change, there's no guaranteed seat.

What we do know is this. Clear, specific information about what you do. Consistency across every place your business is listed. Being mentioned by other credible sources, not just your own site.

Those are the levers that exist. Pulling them doesn't guarantee the name, but ignoring them guarantees you won't be considered.

Worth a conversation if you want to know where you stand.
